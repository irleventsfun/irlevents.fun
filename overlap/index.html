THE OVERLAP
A real-life relationship fit tool

You can love someone and still be a bad daily-life match.

The Overlap shows where your real values sit, how far you can honestly flex, and which differences are likely to become the same fight in a different outfit.

For couples, single people, and anyone tired of dating potential instead of fit.

Who sets the temperature?The person least comfortable with your natural setting often changes the tone first. That is not always bad. The question is whether meeting them is easy—or whether one person keeps disappearing to keep the peace.
What this actually helps with

Stop asking “Are we compatible?” Ask “What would daily life cost us?”

You are not looking for one person to meet every emotional, social, sexual, practical, and personal need. That setup makes nearly everyone fail. The goal is to learn which needs belong inside the partnership, which can be supported elsewhere, and which differences are true dealbreakers.

01

Tell the truth

Answer from your actual habits—not who you become during the first three months of dating.

02

Compare the cost

A difference can be large but easy to handle. A small difference can still hurt if the flex always falls on one person.

03

Try it for 14 days

Use a small agreement, a prepared script, and a fair conversation timer. Then decide using evidence instead of promises.

The relationship temperature

Your natural state is how you live when nobody is managing you. The person least comfortable there will usually ask for change first. Sometimes you can meet that need with almost no effort. Trouble begins when their discomfort becomes every rule, while your ability to flex is treated like you do not have needs at all.

The Overlap
A reflection and communication tool—not therapy, a diagnosis, or a safety assessment. Consent, bodily autonomy, and personal safety are never compromises.
